Theoretical Background

Geometric Brownian Motion:

dSₜ = μSₜdt + σSₜdWₜ

Euler-Maruyama Scheme:

Ŝᵢ₊₁ = Ŝᵢ + μŜᵢh + σŜᵢ√h·Zᵢ

• Strong order: 1/2
• Weak order: 1
• Simple implementation

Milstein Scheme:

Ŝᵢ₊₁ = Ŝᵢ + μŜᵢh + σŜᵢ√h·Zᵢ + ½σ²Ŝᵢ((Zᵢ)² - 1)h

• Strong order: 1
• Weak order: 1
• Higher accuracy for same step size

Convergence Properties

Strong Convergence

E[|Ŝ - S|] ≤ Chᵞ

  • Measures path-wise accuracy
  • Euler: γ = 1/2
  • Milstein: γ = 1

Weak Convergence

|E[f(Ŝ)] - E[f(S)]| ≤ Chᵞ

  • Measures distribution accuracy
  • Important for option pricing
  • Both schemes: γ = 1

Reference:

Chen, N., & Hong, L. J. (2008). Monte Carlo simulation in financial engineering. Proceedings of the 2007 Winter Simulation Conference.

Exact Simulation Methods

Acceptance-Rejection Sampling

Advanced method to generate exact samples from SDE solutions without discretization error.

  • Uses Brownian bridge construction
  • Eliminates discretization bias
  • Computationally intensive
  • Requires bounded drift conditions

Key Steps:

  1. Transform SDE to unit volatility
  2. Apply Girsanov theorem
  3. Use Poisson process for acceptance
  4. Simulate Brownian bridges

Based on Beskos and Roberts (2005) exact simulation methodology
