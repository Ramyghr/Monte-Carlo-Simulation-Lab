Theoretical Foundation

Monte Carlo Methods

Core Principle

Monte Carlo simulation estimates expectations by generating random samples and computing averages:

E[f(X)] ≈ (1/N) ∑ f(Xᵢ)

Convergence Properties

By the Law of Large Numbers, estimates converge to true values as N → ∞. The error decreases as O(1/√N) regardless of problem dimension.

Error ~ Constant / √N

Financial Applications

  • Derivative pricing under risk-neutral measure
  • Portfolio risk measurement (VaR, CVaR)
  • Price sensitivity analysis (Greeks)
  • Credit risk and counterparty exposure
  • Real options and investment decisions

Stochastic Processes in Finance

Geometric Brownian Motion

The standard model for stock prices in the Black-Scholes framework:

dSₜ = μSₜdt + σSₜdWₜ

Risk-Neutral Valuation

Derivative prices are expectations under the risk-neutral measure Q:

V(t,s) = Eᴼ[e^{-r(T-t)} Φ(S_T) | S_t = s]
