Barrier Option Types

Knock-Out Options

  • Up-and-Out: Cease to exist if price rises above barrier
  • Down-and-Out: Cease to exist if price falls below barrier
  • Cheaper than vanilla options
  • Common for range-bound markets

Knock-In Options

  • Up-and-In: Become active if price rises above barrier
  • Down-and-In: Become active if price falls below barrier
  • Cheaper upfront cost
  • Activate only in certain market conditions

Pricing Considerations

Monitoring Frequency

Continuous monitoring vs discrete monitoring affects prices. More frequent monitoring increases knock-out probability.

Barrier Position

  • Barrier above spot: More expensive for knock-outs
  • Barrier below spot: Cheaper for knock-outs
  • At-the-money barriers: Highest sensitivity

Rebates

Some barrier options pay rebates if knocked out, increasing their value compared to non-rebate versions.

Applications & Strategies

Hedging Strategies

  • Cost Reduction: Use knock-outs to reduce hedging costs when certain price levels are unlikely
  • View-Based: Use knock-ins for cheaper exposure to expected market moves
  • Range Trading: Combine knock-outs with other instruments for range-bound strategies

Market Conditions

  • High Volatility: Higher knock-out probabilities, cheaper premiums
  • Trending Markets: Careful barrier placement crucial
  • Sideways Markets: Ideal for knock-out options

Note: Barrier options are sensitive to discrete monitoring and may face "window dressing" effects near barrier levels.
