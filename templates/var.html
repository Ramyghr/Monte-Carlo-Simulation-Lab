VaR Methodology

Definition

Value at Risk (VaR) is the maximum loss not exceeded with a given probability over a given period of time.

P(L > VaR_α) = 1 - α

Monte Carlo Simulation

  • Simulate portfolio returns using Geometric Brownian Motion
  • Calculate losses for each simulation
  • Sort losses and find α-quantile
  • Conditional VaR averages losses beyond VaR

Mathematical Foundation:

Portfolio value: V_T = V₀ exp((μ - σ²/2)Δt + σ√Δt·Z)
Loss: L = V₀ - V_T

Risk Interpretation

99% VaR Example

"We are 99% confident that we will not lose more than $X over the next N days."

Conditional VaR (CVaR)

Also known as Expected Shortfall. Average loss in the worst (1-α)% of cases. Provides better tail risk assessment than VaR alone.

Regulatory Context

Basel Accords require banks to calculate VaR for market risk capital requirements. Typically use 99% confidence over 10-day horizon.

Advanced VaR Methods

Importance Sampling

Variance reduction technique for efficient tail risk estimation, especially useful for high confidence levels (99%, 99.9%).

Delta-Gamma Approximation

Q = a + bᵀZ + ZᵀΛZ
Uses portfolio Greeks for efficient importance sampling

  • Exponentially twist the distribution
  • Focus sampling on tail region
  • Dramatically reduces variance for rare events
  • Asymptotically optimal for large losses

Practical Considerations

Advantages

  • Intuitive and widely accepted
  • Captures nonlinearities and complex dependencies
  • Works with any return distribution
  • Flexible for different time horizons

Limitations

  • Does not measure worst-case loss
  • Assumes normal market conditions
  • Model risk in distribution assumptions
  • Computationally intensive for large portfolios

Reference: Glasserman, Heidelberger & Shahabuddin (2000) - "Variance reduction techniques for estimating value-at-risk"
