Calculate Value at Risk and Conditional Value at Risk using Monte Carlo simulation. Based on the risk management framework from Chen and Hong (2008) and Glasserman et al. (2000). VaR measures the maximum potential loss at a given confidence level.

📖 VaR Interpretation

99% VaR Example

"We are 99% confident that we will not lose more than $X over the next N days."

Conditional VaR (CVaR)

Also known as Expected Shortfall. Average loss in the worst (1-α)% of cases. Provides better tail risk assessment than VaR alone.

Regulatory Context

Basel Accords require banks to calculate VaR for market risk capital requirements. Typically use 99% confidence over 10-day horizon.

VaR Methodology

Mathematical Definition

Value at Risk is the maximum loss not exceeded with probability α:

P(L > VaR_α) = 1 - α

Where L is the portfolio loss and α is the confidence level

Monte Carlo Approach

  • 1. Simulate portfolio returns using GBM
  • 2. Calculate losses for each simulation
  • 3. Sort losses in ascending order
  • 4. Find α-quantile for VaR
  • 5. Average tail losses for CVaR

Portfolio Loss Model

V_T = V₀ exp((μ - σ²/2)Δt + σ√Δt·Z) Loss = V₀ - V_T

Based on Geometric Brownian Motion assumption

Advanced Methods

Importance Sampling

Variance reduction for tail risk estimation, especially useful for high confidence levels.

Delta-Gamma Approximation

Q = a + bᵀZ + ZᵀΛZ

Uses portfolio Greeks for efficient sampling

  • • Exponentially twist distribution
  • • Focus sampling on tail region
  • • Reduces variance for rare events
  • • Asymptotically optimal

Practical Considerations

Advantages

  • • Intuitive and widely accepted
  • • Captures nonlinearities
  • • Works with any distribution
  • • Flexible time horizons

Limitations

  • • Doesn't measure worst-case
  • • Assumes normal conditions
  • • Model risk in assumptions
  • • Computationally intensive
